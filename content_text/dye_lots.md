# What Are Dye Lots, and Why Do They Matter?

If you've ever bought yarn for a big project across two different shopping trips and noticed the colours didn't *quite* match (even though the label said they were the exact same colourway) you've run into dye lots. It's one of those things that can wreck an otherwise perfect project, so it's worth understanding before you buy.

## What a dye lot actually is

Yarn is dyed in batches. Every time a manufacturer dyes a new batch of a particular colour, that batch gets its own **dye lot number**, usually printed on the yarn's label alongside the colour name or number.

Even when a manufacturer uses the exact same recipe every time, small variables: the temperature of the water, the exact concentration of dye, the specific batch of raw fiber, even the weather on the day of dyeing... mean no two batches come out *identically*. Most of the time the difference is subtle, but sometimes it's obvious. Either way, yarn from one dye lot is not guaranteed to be a perfect colour match to yarn from another dye lot, even if both skeins say "Sapphire Blue #114" on the label.

## Why it matters for your project

For small projects like amigurumi, accents, stripes... dye lot differences usually don't matter much, since the eye barely registers small variation. But for anything with large blocks of a single solid colour like sweaters, blankets, cosplay panels or anything where one shade covers a wide, continuous area, a dye lot mismatch can show up as a visible line or shift in tone right across your work. It's especially noticeable in solid stockinette or single crochet, where there's no texture or pattern to disguise the change.

## How to avoid dye lot problems

- **Buy all your yarn for a project at once, from the same dye lot.** Check the dye lot number on every skein before you buy. Most yarn labels have it printed clearly, often near the colour name.
- **Buy a little extra.** Running out mid-project and needing to source more of the same dye lot later is a common problem, since stores sell through specific lots and may not restock the identical one
- **Alternate skeins in striping sections if you're unsure.** Some crafters work with two skeins at once, alternating rows, to blend out any subtle dye lot variation across large areas. This is a technique sometimes called "fading" a mismatch.
- **Check under consistent lighting.** Dye lot differences can be hard to spot under warm indoor lighting but obvious in daylight. When in doubt, compare skeins side by side near a window.

## How this connects to colour matching

When you're using YarnPalette to find a yarn match, keep in mind that the colour you see for a given colourway represents that yarn's *typical* shade, which is not a guarantee for the exact dye lot you'll receive. Once you've found your match, it's worth double-checking the dye lot number on the actual skeins you buy, especially for larger, solid-colour projects.
